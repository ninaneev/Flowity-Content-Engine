# Call It! — Issue #5
## The signal your best Q3 customer already sent you. In Q2.

---

Most founders enter Q3 with a plan.

Goals set. Priorities listed. A few assumptions dressed up as strategy.

What almost none of them carry into that plan is a read of what their customers were actually communicating in the months just before.

Not the dashboard numbers. Not the renewal rate. Not the NPS score.

The actual signals. The behavioral ones. The ones that arrived before any metric moved.

---

Q3 is when a lot of things accelerate — hiring decisions, budget conversations, platform evaluations, vendor reassessments. Which means Q3 is also when patterns from Q2 either compound quietly or surface loudly.

The founders who respond well are rarely the ones who reacted fastest.

They are the ones who read the signal earlier.

---

**The Q3 Signal Audit ✔️**

Before your Q3 rhythm fully kicks in, sit with these:

✅ Which customers expanded their relationship with you in Q2 — and what did they do *differently* from the ones who stayed flat?

✅ Which customers became quieter over the last 60 days? Not angry. Not churned. Just... quieter.

✅ What question did you hear from multiple customers that you mentally filed as "interesting" but never acted on?

✅ Were there any deals that went cold in Q2 that had something in common — an objection, a timing issue, a decision-maker dynamic?

✅ Did any customer say something in passing — in a check-in call, a support reply, a DM — that felt like a hint about what they actually needed?

If the answer to most of these is "I'd have to dig" — this issue is for you.

---

**Five Ways Founders Misread Customer Signals Entering a New Quarter**

📊 **Treating the close rate as the signal.**
Close rate is an outcome. The signal is the objection pattern, the friction in the evaluation, the question every prospect asked before deciding. Revenue tells you what happened. Objections tell you what's starting.

🔇 **Calling reduced engagement "a slow summer."**
A customer who logs in less, responds slower, or skips their check-in isn't just having a busy week. They are showing you something about how embedded your product or service actually is. Seasonal slowdowns mask weak adoption. The signal is there whether you name it or not.

🔄 **Tracking top customers and ignoring the middle tier.**
Your top customers are vocal. Your churned customers are gone. The ones worth watching are the ones in the middle — engaged enough to stay, not invested enough to expand. The future of your Q3 retention is sitting in that cohort right now.

📆 **Waiting for the QBR to read the signal.**
By the time a customer concern shows up on a quarterly business review agenda, it has already been a signal for weeks. The founders who retain customers best are reading the signal in the run-up — not presenting it back to the customer as a structured slide.

🧠 **Confusing "no news" with good news.**
A customer who gives you no friction, no complaints, no questions, and no feature requests is not always satisfied. Sometimes they've mentally moved on and are just waiting for the right moment to say so. Silence is not a signal of health. It requires active interpretation.

---

**What Reading the Signal in Q3 Actually Buys You**

A founder running a B2B advisory practice built her entire Q3 retention strategy around one pattern she noticed in June.

Five clients had each mentioned, in passing, some version of: "I want to be more intentional about how I use our sessions."

Not a complaint. Not a cancellation signal. Just a comment.

Most advisors would have heard that and moved on. She wrote it down. Then noticed she'd heard variations of it across all five clients within the same two-week window.

What she called it: a **scope signal.** Her clients were growing. Their needs were evolving past the original engagement structure. They weren't unhappy — they were ready for a next level.

What she did:

📌 Mapped which clients had said it and where they were in the engagement timeline

📌 Scheduled intentional scope conversations — framed as "evolution check-ins," not upsells

📌 Introduced a tiered structure she'd been sitting on but hadn't launched because "it felt too early"

📌 By September, three of those five clients had moved into an expanded structure

The signal was quiet. It arrived as a polite, off-the-cuff comment. It showed up across five different people in two weeks.

That's not a coincidence. That's a pattern that was asking to be named.

**The Signal Your Q3 Plan Doesn't Know About Yet**

Here's the question worth sitting with as you enter the next quarter:

What did your customers do in Q2 — not say, but *do* — that you haven't translated into a decision yet?

- The ones who used a specific feature more than usual before expanding
- The ones who went quiet exactly 30 days into a new engagement
- The ones who asked for the same thing three different ways before finally dropping it
- The ones who brought in a colleague to a call out of nowhere

These aren't anecdotes. They're patterns waiting for someone to name them.

That is the Q3 signal your plan is probably missing.

**👀 Signals Worth Watching**

**The "we're simplifying our stack" conversation is back.** After two years of aggressive tool adoption, founders are reporting a different kind of pressure in vendor conversations: not "can your tool do X?" but "is this tool something we actually need?" Platform consolidation is accelerating. If your product or service isn't clearly embedded in a core workflow, Q3 is the quarter founders deprioritize it.

**Founder burnout is peaking in June/July — and showing up in customer signals.** Research consistently shows June and January as the highest-burnout months for solopreneurs. What that means for customer signals: decisions slow down, engagement drops, response times extend. If your customers seem harder to reach right now, it's not about you. But it is worth naming and accounting for in your Q3 expectations.

**LinkedIn's algorithm is still rewarding depth over frequency.** Accounts posting 2–3 substantive pieces per week continue to outperform high-frequency posters in organic reach. The pattern has held since April. If you've been wondering whether to post less and think more per post — yes.

**The best Q3 question isn't "what's your goal?" — it's "what's your assumption?"** The founders setting themselves up to read Q3 clearly are the ones writing down their assumptions about customers, market, and team *before* Q3 starts. Not goals — assumptions. So they can catch the divergence when it shows up instead of being surprised by it in October.
